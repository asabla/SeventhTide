# Art direction

The first study uses maritime copperplate engravings, black water, muted sea green, and a single warm light. Use silhouettes that remain clear on a phone. Keep all names, rules, and costs in the interface so a balance change does not require another illustration.

The originals were created with the built-in image generator. Keep each full-size PNG in this directory with its final prompt. The game uses the JPEG exports below.

## First water

The initial page and the Phaser scene use the same 1200 × 800 export. [Original PNG](first-water.png) · [Game JPEG](../web/assets/first-water.jpg)

> Create a single wide landscape illustration for Seventh Tide, a maritime folk-horror tabletop card game. An antique copperplate engraving printed with carbon-black ink, pale bone and desaturated sea-green on worn paper. A tiny awkward lashed timber raft with two exhausted oilskin-clad figures and a single small amber lantern, a third sailor swimming tethered alongside reaching for a sealed ration tin bobbing in foam. A distant sinking steamship almost swallowed by a pale fog bank; inky curling crosshatched swells in foreground. Readable bold silhouettes, generous pale sky, intricate hand-engraved linework. Museum print quality, gritty and restrained, no typography, no lettering, no borders, no UI. Landscape 3:2, frame the raft and swimmer together near the center to allow a wide crop.

## Salvage

The 960 × 640 export contains three equal-width panels. From left to right they show the ration locker, canvas, and spar. [Original PNG](salvage.png) · [Game JPEG](../web/assets/salvage.jpg)

> A single antique maritime engraving specimen plate, landscape 3:2, designed to split into three equal-width vertical panels. In each third, ONE isolated object centered on the same warm bone lightly weathered blank paper. Left third: a tall stack of three sealed cylindrical old plain ration tins bound by cord. Middle third: a compact roll of dark sail canvas tied with twine. Right third: two broken wooden ship spars crossed and coiled with rope. Each object fits within its own third with generous blank margins on all sides. Use carbon-black and muted sea-green crosshatched engraved linework, dark outlines, any rust restrained amber, dramatic museum quality copperplate illustration as for a grim 1890 nautical survival card game. No letters, words, labels, frame, separators, backgrounds, people, UI or watermark. All three objects have strong individual silhouettes at small size.

## Sound the depths and Haul the line

The paired card engravings use the rope to connect the swimmer below the surface with the sailor on the raft. Each game export is 180 × 180 pixels.

The generator received this common prompt followed by the scene for each card.

> Square copperplate engraving for the 1890 maritime horror card game Seventh Tide. Carbon black and muted sea-green ink on weathered bone paper. Bold readable silhouette framed close for a small phone card, finely cut lines and restrained stippling. No letters, border, title, watermark or UI.

**Sound the depths** · [Original PNG](sound-the-depths.png) · [Game JPEG](../web/assets/sound-the-depths.jpg)

> From beneath the surface a tethered oilskin sailor leans forward to peer down into the dark water, one hand gripping a knotted hemp line descending diagonally from upper left. The pale underside of a swell hangs overhead. A faint impossible eye shape in the crosshatching far below.

**Haul the line** · [Original PNG](haul-the-line.png) · [Game JPEG](../web/assets/haul-the-line.jpg)

> From raft level an exhausted sailor crouches on the lashed timbers, braced boots against rough wood, both hands hauling a taut knotted hemp line angling over the lower right edge into dark water. A small amber lantern glows behind one shoulder. The gesture and taut rope dominate.

## The answering light

The built-in image generator produced `answering-light.png`. The scene uses `web/assets/answering-light.jpg` from tide six onward.

> Create a wide landscape 3:2 antique maritime copperplate engraving for Seventh Tide. From the waterline, two answering lanterns amid a fogbound channel: a steady amber lamp on the prow of a rescue cutter, and a second pale lamp drifting against a curling wake with no visible vessel beneath it. Put both lights close to the centre, horizontally separated, at mid-height so they read in a shallow banner and a phone crop. Dark carbon-black engraved waves and muted sea-green ink on worn bone paper, bold silhouettes, fine etched hatching, restrained amber. No lettering, borders or interface.

## Equipment hand study

The built-in generator produced the scene in `raft-table.png`. The hand study uses `web/assets/raft-table.webp` for the table and a blank margin from the salvage original for `web/assets/log-paper.webp`.

> A widescreen 16:9 background illustration edge to edge for a maritime tabletop horror game, Seventh Tide. A PLAYER'S FIRST PERSON VIEW looking down at a rough maritime gaming surface on a tiny drifting raft in a 1890 storm. Antique densely inked copperplate engraving with aquatint tone, luminous fine carved white lines in black ink and muted petrol and verdigris on tarnished bone paper. TOP QUARTER: hostile open cold water, a tiny far off half sunk steamship slightly right of center, pale scratched fog, and an impossible parallel wake through the water, no monster. LOWER THREE QUARTERS: steeply downward view of broad real black salt worn raft timbers close to the viewer, rough dark weathered long wood boards spanning the composition with layered harsh uneven grain, a little shallow spray catching reflected ochre light. The middle playing surface is clear dark wood and open space. On the far RIGHT edge, a large hand-engraved brass and glass hurricane lantern burns with a small amber flame, ragged thick rope coil below it on the far edge, damp black cloth and a large corroded nail. On the far left edge a single diagonal lash of thick fraying hemp. Directional light from right, total darkness in corners. Distinct tactile lighting and acid-etched texture, monumental silhouettes, cold and lonely, gritty maritime survival. Highly detailed fine hand-cut linework. No people, lettering, printed numbers, cards, interface, boxes, borders or words. Compose as an actual full-bleed scene, keep central and left parts empty of objects for overlaying actual interactive game pieces.

The built-in image generator produced `boathook.png` as a paired plate. The left and right halves supply `web/assets/boathook-raft.jpg` and `web/assets/boathook-water.jpg`. The new 560-pixel Sounding line exports use the existing Haul the line and Sound the depths originals.

> Create a two-panel copperplate engraving, two equal square woodcut-style pictures edge to edge on one 2:1 landscape sheet for Seventh Tide, a grim 1890 maritime folk horror card game. Each square shows the SAME distinctive iron boathook with a single curved side prong, blunt nose, bright worn ferrule and blackened ash shaft. The hook head must be large and plainly recognizable in the middle of BOTH panels at postage stamp size. LEFT, above water: a sailor's raw gripping hand and oilskin sleeve thrust the pole diagonally to wedge its iron head between two lashed, splintering raft timbers. RIGHT, waterline: the same pole catches a separate pale floating beam from underneath, foam around the timber and a swimmer's soaked hand on the shaft. Deliberately different compositions, strong silhouettes, close framing, restrained crosshatching, hand-engraved linework on weathered bone paper, dense carbon black ink and muted sea-green washes. Small pits of rust. Oppressive stillness, barely perceptible parallel wake in the right scene. Flat print scan. No typography, borders, symbols, divider, watermark, extra hook or complete monster.
